## Cycle 1 — 2026-09-22

### Summary of the paper (as this reviewer understands it)

A purpose-built Next.js/Zod testbed with 43 written business rules (5 enforced) receives inputs from
seven generation conditions on 17 targets. The harness records schema and HTTP verdicts. Semantic labels
come from 11 objective rule functions and a gemma3:4b judge. The judge was run on a stratified sample; human
annotation is still pending. Claims: H1 supported (D∪E conditional SV-SI 28.0 %); H2 supported
(χ²=214, V=0.33); H3a–c not supported, and random inputs exceed model inputs within target (post hoc CMH);
H5 not supported (best non-judge recall 26.9 % at the FPR ≤ 0.05 operating point).

### What was verified

- Recomputed from `raw/E1_E1_20260920T0847/*.jsonl` independently of the scripts: H1 344/1228 = 28.0 %
  [25.6, 30.6]; per-condition decided counts and rates (Table I); category rates (Table II); per-run
  rates; all five CMH odds ratios and CIs; the AI-facing 134/20/114 split; the retried-sensitivity 27.6 %.
  All match the prose and tables.

  byte-identical to the ones in the draft. Ran `e3_analysis.py` in a scratch folder: `table6*.csv` are
  identical. The E2b prose matches `table7*.csv` and RESULTS_LOG.
- Every number quoted in §VI-A to §VI-F matches its analysis output. The problems below are in what those
  outputs measure, not in how they were transcribed.

### Strengths

- The pre-registered negative results (H3, H5) are reported plainly. Deviations DV-01 to DV-13 are logged
  with preserved invalid data. The DV-11 threshold bug was caught before any detector figure was reported.
- The schema and HTTP verdicts are recorded separately. Paired E3 comparison on a shared sample.
  The circularity of R is disclosed.

### Simulated recommendation: **Major revision** (close to Reject until I-R1-001 is fixed)

Main reasons:
1. A harness defect (I-R1-001) replaced 365 model outputs with the fixture payload and counted them as
   valid model inputs. Every D/E figure must be recomputed. Directions probably survive (H1 rises to
   31.7 %), but the magnitudes in the paper are not correct.
2. For about 29 % of violations the judge had no access to the context its rules refer to (I-R1-002).
   That includes the whole "structured" category, which drives the RQ2 interpretation.
3. The headline rate is set largely by design choices that are not disclosed as such: rule-violation
   prompt families (I-R1-003), a date the model was never given (I-R1-004) and unweighted sampling
   (I-R1-007). The framing "rules the schema could not express" is contradicted by the testbed's own
   `.refine` checks (I-R1-005).
4. The transcription of numbers is accurate. The negative results (H3, H5) survive every sensitivity
   check I ran. The paper can be saved once the population, labels and claims are corrected.
