## 3. HONEST ASSESSMENT

No paper found does the full combination "structural-vs-semantic validity gap of LLM-generated inputs in a web application + input-source comparison (LLM/random/rule-based/human) + measured multi-tier semantic layer with latency". However, the core phenomenon is no longer novel as a finding: at least five 2026 preprints independently report "100% schema-valid yet semantically wrong" and argue for separating the two metrics — T1 (OrderBench, closest: structured ordering objects, unsafe schema-valid acceptances), T2 (Constraint Tax: "wrong-valid-schema rate"), T3 (SOB), T4 ("interface misuse vs semantic misuse"), T5 (production enterprise workflows: "separate structural validity from semantic correctness"), T6 (syntax/structural/semantic taxonomy). T8 (CHI EA 2026) is the closest in framing to the web domain — "semantic violations that pass automated checks" in LLM-generated web UIs, detected with a fault-injection-validated LLM judge — but concerns accessibility markup, not application input data. Latency-measured layered validators exist for prompt-injection safety (T11: 61.2 ms; T12: embeddings 37.6 ms vs LLM-judge 250–900 ms) and neuro-symbolic rules+embeddings verification exists in a medical setting (T10). Novelty therefore rests on: (i) the web-application input/persistence setting and business-rule constraints, (ii) the controlled comparison with non-LLM input sources (no such comparison surfaced in 26 queries), and (iii) the head-to-head cost/accuracy/latency evaluation of the four semantic-layer designs. Novelty risk for "the gap exists" claim: HIGH (must be positioned as replication/extension citing T1–T6); risk for the setting + comparison + layer evaluation: MEDIUM. Recommend the team read T1, T2, T4, T5 and T8 in full (V4) before Gate A, and re-run this check monthly: this area produced roughly one relevant preprint per month in Mar–Sep 2026.
